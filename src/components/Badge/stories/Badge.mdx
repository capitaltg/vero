<DocsWhenToUse>

<DocsWhenToUseCard kind="use">

- **To label an item's status or category**, such as "Approved" or "Draft" in a table row.
- **To show a count**, such as the number of unread messages next to a link.
- **To draw attention to new or important content**, such as "New" next to a recently added
  feature.

</DocsWhenToUseCard>

<DocsWhenToUseCard kind="avoid">

- **Users can click it:** use a [Button](?path=/docs/actions-navigation-button--docs). A badge
  isn't interactive: it can't be focused and has no button role.
- **It's a message users need to read or act on:** use an
  [Alert](?path=/docs/overlays-feedback-alert--docs).
- **To highlight new content on a page users expect to change often**, such as a news feed: putting
  the new items first may be enough. Bold text or a background color can also mark them.

</DocsWhenToUseCard>

</DocsWhenToUse>

<DocsSubheading id="usability">Usability guidance</DocsSubheading>

- **Don't put badges where they look like buttons.** Users often mistake them for buttons,
  especially next to real ones. Test with users if you're unsure.
- **Keep the text short**: one or two words, or a number.
- **Use each variant for one meaning across your app**, such as `success` for "Approved"
  everywhere, so users learn what the colors mean.
- **Don't overdo it.** If everything has a badge, nothing stands out.
- **Use the `light` tone for a quieter badge**, such as in a dense table, and `dark` where it needs
  to stand out.

</DocsSection>

<DocsSection
  id="accessibility"
  intro="What the badge handles for you and what you need to add."
  title="Accessibility"
>

<DocsSubheading id="built-in">Built in</DocsSubheading>

- **It's plain text to assistive technology.** The badge is a `<div>` with no role, so screen
  readers read its text where it appears, like any other text. Its variant and color aren't
  announced.
- **It isn't focusable**, so it doesn't add a stop for keyboard users.
- **Contrast:** the text is at least 4.6:1 against the badge's background in every variant and
  tone, above the 4.5:1 that WCAG requires for small text. The lowest are the `dark` `success` and
  `danger` badges; every `light` badge is at least 5.6:1.
- **In Windows High Contrast mode** every badge shows as an outlined pill with its text, so badges
  stay visible, but all variants look the same.

<DocsSubheading id="your-responsibility">Your responsibility</DocsSubheading>

- **Put the meaning in the text, not just the color.** Write "Approved" or "Overdue", not a colored
  badge with a vague word. Screen readers don't announce the color, some users can't tell the colors
  apart, and in Windows High Contrast mode every variant looks the same.
- **Give counts some context.** A badge reading "3" next to "Messages" is read as "Messages 3". Add
  visually hidden text, such as "unread" in a span with Tailwind's `sr-only` class, so it's clear
  what the number means.
- **Announce badges that change while the page is open**, such as a count that goes up. The badge
  has no live region: put the change in an `aria-live` region, or announce it some other way.
- **Check the contrast of custom colors** passed in `className`: text needs 4.5:1 against the
  badge's background.
- **Test it in your page.** Run the
  [USWDS tag accessibility tests](https://designsystem.digital.gov/components/tag/accessibility-tests/)
  on your own page, including a screen reader pass.

For an accessibility review, see
[all open accessibility issues for Badge](https://github.com/capitaltg/vero/issues?q=is%3Aopen+label%3Aa11y+in%3Atitle+Badge).
